📊The record

🔧Corrections in force

Where the board says HAWK runs high or low, its chances are shifted by this much before you ever see them. A gap above is therefore not money leaking — it is the measurement the correction is made from.

⏳Measured, not trusted

A market only gets its correction once HAWK has graded every leg twice — once raw, once corrected — and the corrected one has actually scored better. These haven't earned it: either too few legs yet, or the correction made them worse and was withheld. The figure is the legs so far, or the Brier units the shift lost.

💷Would it have paid?

A flat 1 unit on every leg that closed with a price, sorted by how much value HAWK claimed at that price. The right-hand cards are the test — if backing what HAWK calls value doesn't return more than backing what it calls bad, the claim is decoration. These are single legs at the closing price, which is the kindest case on both counts: a single carries far less margin than a builder, and the closing price is the sharpest number of the day. Nothing clearing here can be rescued by putting it in a builder.

🎯Where it paid

The same ledger per market, counting only legs HAWK priced at 2%+ value. This is the cell a selection rule would read. The figure in brackets is every leg in that market, valued or not — a market that pays only when HAWK picks it is worth something, one that pays either way is just a loose market.

⚖️Reading the price

Stripping a bookmaker's margin by dividing each chance by their sum is the simple way, and it is what HAWK does everywhere. It leans: too much probability left on the long price, too little on the short one — HAWK's own result record has the draw landing 2.2 points under what it said. Power and Shin are two reads that correct for it, scored here against the same legs and the same outcomes. The ladder is the part that matters, because a per-market average can't show a difference: both sides of a market are graded, so they average to exactly half whatever the prices were. Long prices sit in the bottom band. If the three agree there, there is nothing to switch for.
